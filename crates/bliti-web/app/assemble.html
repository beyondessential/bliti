For provisioning

Download the image
Size
about 7.2 GB to download, 17.8 GB once written
Checksum
tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst.sha256
Manifest
tamanu-iti-provision.txt

Rebuilt every week. Always download a fresh image for a batch.

Check it, then write it to the stick

Write the stick from a computer. A phone or tablet can't.

Check the download

Download the checksum file too, into the same folder, then run this in PowerShell in that folder.

(Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 .\tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst).Hash -eq (Get-Content .\tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst.sha256).Split(' ')[0]

It prints True when the download is intact. Otherwise, download the image again.

Raspberry Pi Imager

  1. Install Raspberry Pi Imager and open it.
  2. Choose any device, then under operating system choose Use custom and pick the downloaded .img.zst file.
  3. Choose the USB stick as the storage, check it is the right one, and write.

balenaEtcher

Etcher can't read .zst files, so unpack the download first. The unpacked image takes 17.8 GB of free disk.

  1. Unpack the download with zstd -d tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst, which leaves tamanu-iti-provision.img beside it. Install zstd first with brew install zstd.
  2. Install balenaEtcher and open it.
  3. Choose Flash from file and pick the unpacked .img, then Select target and pick the USB stick, and flash.

Or the terminal

This replaces everything on the disk you name, so check its name twice. Find the stick, with zstd installed (brew install zstd):

diskutil list external
diskutil unmountDisk /dev/diskN
zstd -dc tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst | sudo dd of=/dev/rdiskN bs=4m

Replace N with the stick's number. dd prints nothing until it finishes; press Ctrl+T to see how far it has got.

GNOME Disks

Disks comes with GNOME. Unpack the download first, which takes 17.8 GB of free disk.

  1. Unpack the download with zstd -d tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst, which leaves tamanu-iti-provision.img beside it.
  2. Open Disks and select the USB stick in the list of drives.
  3. Open the drive's menu and choose Restore Disk Image, pick the unpacked .img, and start restoring.

zstd -dc tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst | sudo dd of=/dev/sdX bs=4M conv=fsync status=progress

Replace sdX with the stick, as lsblk lists it.

Assembly

  1. Unscrew the lid from the case, and take out what is inside.

    The case ships closed, with its parts inside. Keep the four lid screws: they screw the lid back on at the end, and they are the same as the screws that fasten the assembly to the bottom of the case.

  2. Stick the four feet on the bottom of the case.
  3. Fit the Active Cooler to the Pi and plug it in.
  4. Plug the RTC cell into the Pi. Leave it outside for now.
  5. Fit the four spacers to the Pi.
    Use
    • 4 × brass female-female spacers from the X1208 pack
    • 4 × brass M2.5 × 12+6 mm female-male spacers from the case pack
  6. Thread the RTC cell with its wires between the white plastic post of the Active Cooler and the spacer.

    This keeps the wires clear of the ports and the power button. They snap into place with a tock, which the video has sound for: unmute it to hear it.

  7. Connect the PCIe flat cable to the Pi.

    Plug the end marked "RPi5" into the Pi, with the golden contacts facing inwards.

  8. Seat the X1208 board on the Pi, with the pogo pin between them.

    Do all of this with the X1208 board upside down, until the pogo pin fits snugly and can no longer fall out.

    1. The pogo pin has a flat end and a pointy end. Put the flat end into the hole marked Pi PWR, from the underside of the X1208 board.
    2. Stand the board on the table upside down, with the pin pointing up.
    3. Pass the free end of the PCIe cable, already on the Pi, through the hole in the board, before pressing the boards together.
    4. Lower the Pi onto it. The pointy end of the pin goes into the square hole next to the debug port. This mostly happens by itself as the Pi goes into the header socket.
    5. Squeeze the two boards together firmly until they are well seated.
    Use
    • 1 × pogo pin from the X1208 pack
  9. Connect the PCIe flat cable to the X1208 board.
  10. Screw the board down onto the spacers.
    Use
    • 4 × black M2.5 × 5 mm screws, to fasten the board from the X1208 pack
    photo: which screws, and where
  11. Insert the 21700 cell, the right way round as the X1208 marks.
  12. Fit the cell shim over the cell.

    Press it until it snaps into place. It must stay there, and not fall out afterwards.

  13. Peel the cover off the RTC cell's adhesive pad, and stick the pad to its unlabelled side.
  14. Stick the RTC cell into the slot in the cell shim.
  15. Fit the Tamanu Iti HAT. Its screw goes in the Pi's corner mounting hole that sits nearest the middle of the X1208, in place of the screw already there.

    Line it up carefully. Powering the Iti with the HAT misaligned will probably destroy the HAT.

    1. Unscrew the screw from that corner hole, and put it aside.
    2. Line the HAT up with the Pi's header. Along the HAT there is a 2 × 10 socket, then a block of 2 × 6 holes with no metal in them, then the screw hole. The socket goes onto the middle of the Pi's header, leaving 2 × 4 pins uncovered at the end away from the screw. The holes go over the 2 × 6 pins at the other end, and the screw hole goes over the open mounting hole.
    3. Press the HAT on, and settle it firmly.
    4. Check that 2 × 4 pins of the Pi's header are left uncovered, at the end away from the screw.
    5. Fasten it with the M2.5 × 12 mm screw, which is longer to reach through the HAT.
    Use
    • 1 × silver M2.5 × 12 mm machine screw the one you bought, not from a pack
  16. Stick the heatsink onto the NVMe drive, with its thermal pad.
    1. Peel one clear cover off the thermal pad and stick the pad to the heatsink.
    2. Peel the other cover off and stick the heatsink, pad side down, onto the drive.
  17. Fit the NVMe drive into the X1208 board and screw it down.
    Use
    • 1 × silver M2 × 4 mm drive screw from the X1208 pack
  18. Fit the SD slot shim.

    It sits in the slot like a card, and its tab is trapped by the case so it can't be pulled out once the case is on.

    photo or video
  19. Put the assembly in the case, and screw it to the bottom of the case, one screw into each spacer.
    Use
    • 4 × black M2.5 × 4 mm screws, the same as the lid screws from the case pack
  20. Fit the lid shim to the inside of the lid, over the CASE switch. It hooks on and slides into place.
    1. Hold the shim under the lid, with its hooks up and a little back from where it will end up. Keep it clear of the table, because the bump under it dips as you push.
    2. Push the two hooks up through the long slots in the lid.
    3. Slide the shim along the slots until the bump clicks up into the round hole.

    To remove it, push the bump down through the round hole with a pin, and slide the shim back.

    photo: the hooks in the slots, and the bump in the hole

Provisioning

  1. Plug in the prepared USB stick.
    photo or video
  2. Plug in the network cable.
    photo or video
  3. Plug in the power, in the UPS's port. The Iti boots by itself.
    photo or video
  4. Open the provisioning page in Chrome on a phone or computer, and go through it.

    Provision the Iti

    photo or video
  5. Write the Iti's four-character code on a Post-it, and stick it to the case.

    The provisioning page shows the code.

    photo or video
  6. Shut the Pi down with one press of the UPS's button, and wait until the main LED and the blue SSD LED are both off.
    photo or video
  7. Unplug everything: the USB stick, the network cable and the power.
    photo or video

Closing up

  1. Fit the lid onto the case.
    photo or video
  2. Screw the lid on.
    Use
    • 4 × black M2.5 × 4 mm screws the lid screws, set aside in the first step
    photo: the lid screws
  3. Print the sheet of labels for the whole batch from the provisioning page, on A4 sticker paper.

    Do this once every Iti of the batch is provisioned. The sheet has a label for each of them.

    photo or video
  4. Put the sticker on the bottom of the Iti. Cut out the label with the same four characters as the Post-it on the case, and peel it.
    photo or video
  5. Record the Iti in inventory, using the QR code on its sticker as its serial.
    photo or video
