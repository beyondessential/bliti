Assemble an Iti

Follow this guide to build an Iti from its parts, provision it, and test it. The provisioning image is a large download, so start it first.

Tools and extras

For provisioning

Get the provisioning image

Download the image
Size
about 7.2 GB to download, 17.8 GB once written
Checksum
tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst.sha256
Manifest
tamanu-iti-provision.txt

The image is rebuilt every week. The provisioning page refuses one that is a month old, so download a fresh one rather than reusing a stick you wrote earlier.

Write it to the stick

Write the stick from a computer. A phone or tablet can't.

Raspberry Pi Imager

  1. Install Raspberry Pi Imager and open it.
  2. Choose any device, then under operating system choose Use custom and pick the downloaded .img.zst file.
  3. Choose the USB stick as the storage, check it is the right one, and write.

Check the download first

In PowerShell, in the folder you downloaded to, and compare the result with the text in the .sha256 file:

Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 .\tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst

balenaEtcher

Etcher can't read .zst files, so unpack the download first. The unpacked image takes 17.8 GB of free disk.

  1. Unpack the download with zstd -d tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst, which leaves tamanu-iti-provision.img beside it. Install zstd first with brew install zstd.
  2. Install balenaEtcher and open it.
  3. Choose Flash from file and pick the unpacked .img, then Select target and pick the USB stick, and flash.

Or the terminal

This replaces everything on the disk you name, so check its name twice. Find the stick, with zstd installed (brew install zstd):

diskutil list external
shasum -a 256 -c tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst.sha256
diskutil unmountDisk /dev/diskN
zstd -dc tamanu-iti-provision.img.zst | sudo dd of=/dev/rdiskN bs=4m

Replace N with the stick's number. dd prints nothing until it finishes; press Ctrl+T to see how far it has got.

  2. Open Disks and select the USB stick in the list of drives.
  3. Open the drive's menu and choose Restore Disk Image, pick the unpacked .img, and start restoring.

Replace sdX with the stick, as lsblk lists it.

Assembly

Leave the lid off, with its screws out, until you are told otherwise.

  1. Fit the Active Cooler to the Pi and plug it in.

  2. Plug the RTC cell into the Pi. Leave it outside for now.

  3. Seat the Pi on the X1208 board and screw everything together.

    Use
    • 4 × M2.5 × 13.5 mm spacers X1208 pack
    • 8 × M2.5 × 5 mm screws, four from below into the spacers and four to fasten the board X1208 pack

    from Geekworm's guide, confirm against the pack and this stack

    photo: which screws, and where

  4. Connect the PCIe flat cable between the Pi and the X1208.

  5. Fit the NVMe drive with its heatsink.

    1. Peel one clear cover off the thermal pad and stick the pad to the heatsink.
    2. Peel the other cover off and stick the heatsink, pad side down, onto the drive.
    3. Fit the drive.
    Use
    • 1 × M2 screw to hold the drive X1208 pack size, confirm
    photo: the drive screw

  6. Fit the Tamanu Iti HAT. Its screw goes in the Pi's corner mounting hole that sits nearest the middle of the X1208, in place of the screw already there.

    1. Unscrew the screw from that corner hole, and put it aside.
    2. Press the HAT on, and settle it firmly.
    3. Fasten it with the M2.5 × 12 mm screw, which is longer to reach through the HAT.
    Use
    • 1 × M2.5 × 12 mm machine screw the one you bought, not from a pack
    photo: the hole, and the HAT seated

  7. Fit the SD slot shim.

    It sits in the slot like a card, and its tab is trapped by the case so it can't be pulled out once the case is on.

  8. Thread the RTC cell so it comes out underneath the Pi.

  9. Insert the 21700 cell, the right way round as the X1208 marks, and fit the cell shim over it.

    Technically it goes under it, between the cell and the bottom of the case.

  10. Fix the RTC cell. Stick its adhesive pad to its unlabelled side, then stick it into the slot in the cell shim.

  11. Put the assembly in the case, and screw it to the bottom of the case.

    Use
    • which screws from the case pack, how many, and which holes
    photo: the screws and holes

  12. Leave the lid off, and its four screws out.

Provisioning

  1. Write the provisioning image to the USB stick, as above. No SD card is needed.

  2. Plug in the stick, boot the Pi, and open the provisioning page in Chrome on a phone or computer. It guides you through the rest.

    The page asks you to press the USER button, then the CASE switch, on the HAT. The lid is off so you can reach both.

    Provision the Iti

  3. When it finishes, shut the Pi down how: the Pi's power button? and remove the stick, the network cable and the power, in either order.

    The backup supply keeps the Iti running until it has shut down, so unplugging the power first does no harm.

Closing up

  1. Stick the lid shim to the inside of the lid, where it meets the CASE switch.

  2. Screw the lid on. The Iti is ready.

    Use
    • 4 × screw size case pack
    photo: the lid screws

Testing

Boot the finished Iti and connect to it with the operator app over Bluetooth.

If provisioning didn't set up Tailscale, set it up from the app.

The Case tile in the app's device view should read closed: that confirms the lid presses the CASE switch.

The USER button stays reachable with the case closed. Press it through the case with a thin tool, such as the back of a pair of tweezers.
